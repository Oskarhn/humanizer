# Original worked examples

These examples illustrate decisions, not mandatory phrasings. All examples are newly authored for this project. Revised text may be shorter or longer depending on the task. Keeping a good sentence unchanged is a successful edit.

## 1. Generic business prose with supplied substance

**Brief:** Rewrite the paragraph for an internal decision note. Facts supplied separately: staff currently enter the same order in two systems; a proposed integration would copy it; the prototype has not been tested in production.

**Before:**

> In today's rapidly evolving business landscape, leveraging innovative solutions is pivotal to unlocking efficiency and fostering seamless collaboration across the organization.

**After:**

> Staff enter each order in two systems. The proposed integration would copy the order between them, removing the second entry. The prototype has not been tested in production.

**Why:** Uses the supplied mechanism and preserves the deployment limitation. Without those supplied facts, this rewrite would be fabrication. In an edit-only task with no supporting context, identify the missing substance instead.

## 2. Measured result with uncertainty

**Before:**

> It is important to note that in our pilot of 42 users, the median completion time decreased from 8.4 to 6.9 minutes. These findings may indicate a benefit, although the absence of random assignment means that a causal conclusion cannot be established [7].

**After:**

> In our pilot of 42 users, median completion time fell from 8.4 to 6.9 minutes. The result may indicate a benefit, but without random assignment we cannot establish causation [7].

**Bad edit:** “The intervention made users 18% faster.” That adds a calculation, changes the outcome wording, and asserts causation. A faithful edit preserves the measured endpoint and limitation.

## 3. Keep a useful technical term

**Before:**

> We estimated robust standard errors because residual variance differed across groups.

**After:**

> We estimated robust standard errors because residual variance differed across groups.

**Why:** “Robust” names the method. A blacklist would damage the text.

## 4. Norwegian operational update

**Before:**

> I forbindelse med gjennomføringen av vedlikeholdsarbeidet vil nettsiden være utilgjengelig fra kl. 22.00 til 22.15. Det er viktig å merke seg at allerede innsendte bestillinger ikke vil bli påvirket.

**After:**

> Nettsiden er utilgjengelig fra kl. 22.00 til 22.15 under vedlikeholdet. Bestillinger som allerede er sendt inn, blir ikke påvirket.

**Why:** The source does not identify the maintenance team, so the rewrite does not introduce “vi.” Do not expand a statement about submitted orders into “alle data er trygge.”

## 5. Keep Nynorsk and the unresolved condition

**Before:**

> Vi ønskjer å informere om at vi vil gjennomføre ei evaluering av ordninga i desember. Ei eventuell vidareføring føreset at vi får dekka reisekostnadene.

**After:**

> Vi evaluerer ordninga i desember. Skal ho halde fram, må vi få dekka reisekostnadene.

**Why:** Keeps the written standard and condition. No conversion to Bokmål, invented funding, or guaranteed continuation.

## 6. Real disappointment

**Before:**

> I waited three weeks for the replacement. It arrived broken too. I'm tired of filling in the same form.

**After:**

> I waited three weeks for the replacement. It arrived broken too. I'm tired of filling in the same form.

**Bad edit:** “While the process has presented challenges, I appreciate your continued support.” This replaces a clear complaint with gratitude the writer did not express.

## 7. Specificity that is absent

**Before:**

> The tool significantly improves team productivity through an intuitive and robust workflow.

**Review:**

> What work does the tool remove, and what evidence supports “significantly”? Those details would make the claim useful. The paragraph does not provide them.

**Conservative style-only rewrite:**

> The tool's intuitive, robust workflow significantly improves team productivity.

**Caution:** This slightly simplifies expression but does not solve the unsupported substance. “The tool is intended to improve productivity” would change an asserted result into a goal. Do that only when the context establishes intention or the user authorizes the correction. In style-only work, preserve claim status and raise the evidence question separately when commentary is allowed. The absence of evidence in a paragraph does not prove the claim false.

## 8. No invented autobiography

**Brief:** Make a generic introduction more natural. No personal facts are supplied.

**Before:**

> Learning a new language can be a challenging yet rewarding journey.

**Strict style-only revision:**

> Learning a new language can be difficult and rewarding.

**Why:** Keeps both the possibility and the two-part claim without inventing a personal experience. Adding slow progress, a study result, or a reason for learning would require source support or drafting latitude.

**Bad edit:** “When I moved to Lisbon, I cried after my first Portuguese lesson.” A vivid invented life event does not become acceptable because it sounds human.

## 9. Fiction: trust the action already present

**Before:**

> Mina set the unopened letter under the breadboard. When her brother asked if the post had come, she shook her head. This moment showed the complex ways in which fear can shape our choices and underscored the fragile nature of trust.

**After:**

> Mina set the unopened letter under the breadboard. When her brother asked if the post had come, she shook her head.

**Why:** The existing action carries the tension. The edit removes an external moral summary without inventing motive, backstory, or a new ending. If the source is deliberately didactic narration, a different decision may fit better.

## 10. Preserve the cost

**Before:**

> The smaller unit provides an ideal balance of portability and capability. It fits in the field bag, but its battery lasts only two hours and cannot be replaced on site.

**After:**

> The smaller unit fits in the field bag. Its battery lasts only two hours, though, and cannot be replaced on site.

**Why:** Retains the usable advantage and unresolved disadvantage. Does not convert a real constraint into an “opportunity for optimization.”

## 11. Preserve attribution and negation

**Before:**

> The supplier says the seal is waterproof, but we have not tested that claim. The enclosure passed the vibration test.

**After:**

> The enclosure passed the vibration test. We have not tested the supplier's claim that the seal is waterproof.

**Why:** Keeps two different evidence states separate. “The enclosure passed our waterproofing and vibration tests” would invent a test result.

## 12. Keep useful formatting

**Brief:** Lightly edit an operating checklist.

**Before:**

> 1. Close the inlet valve.
> 2. Wait until the gauge reads 0 bar.
> 3. Disconnect the hose.

**After:** unchanged.

**Why:** Parallel structure, an ordered list, and explicit sequencing serve the task. Breaking them into casual prose would make the instructions harder to use.

## 13. Preserve the user's voice without importing facts

**User's samples:** “Send me the numbers first. We can argue about the slides later.” / “The idea is fine. The deadline isn't.”

**Draft to revise:**

> It would be beneficial to prioritize verification of the costs before proceeding with the presentation update. The proposed timeline has not yet been approved.

**Possible revision:**

> Checking the costs before updating the presentation would help. The proposed timeline has not yet been approved.

**Why:** Borrows compactness without turning a suggestion into an instruction. No approval has been given; that alone does not establish that approval is required. Voice matching does not authorize stronger obligations, copied biography, or an invented rejection.

## 14. Keep a legitimate synthesis

**Before:**

> Across both trials, the improvement appeared only in the subgroup with prior training. The result supports a narrower follow-up study rather than a general rollout.

**After:** unchanged.

**Why:** A conclusion that interprets evidence and guides a decision is useful. The skill does not delete all concluding paragraphs.

## 15. Norwegian technical prose: simulation remains simulation

**Bokmål, before:**

> Det ble gjennomført en simulering av regulatoren. Simuleringen viste 3,28 V ved 0,80 A last. Modellen tar ikke hensyn til temperaturavhengighet. Det er ikke utført en fysisk måling av denne kretsen.

**Bokmål, after:**

> Regulatoren ble simulert. Simuleringen viste 3,28 V ved 0,80 A last. Modellen tar ikke hensyn til temperaturavhengighet. Det er ikke utført en fysisk måling av denne kretsen.

**Nynorsk, before:**

> Det vart gjennomført ei simulering av regulatoren. Simuleringa viste 3,28 V ved 0,80 A last. Modellen tek ikkje omsyn til temperaturavhengnad. Det er ikkje utført ei fysisk måling av denne kretsen.

**Nynorsk, after:**

> Regulatoren vart simulert. Simuleringa viste 3,28 V ved 0,80 A last. Modellen tek ikkje omsyn til temperaturavhengnad. Det er ikkje utført ei fysisk måling av denne kretsen.

**Why:** A small verb repair is sufficient. The values remain simulation results, temperature dependence remains excluded, and physical measurement remains unperformed. [“Krets” and “krins” are both valid Nynorsk forms](https://ordbokene.no/nob/nn/krets); retain the author's consistent choice. “Kretsen er verifisert ved 0,80 A” would invent physical verification. No operator is supplied, so passive voice remains useful.
